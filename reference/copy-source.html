Residential Mortgages

Residential mortgages, explained simply

A residential mortgage is a loan secured against the home you're going to live in. The lender agrees to lend you a set amount, over an agreed term, secured against the property — and you pay it back monthly, usually with interest.

Re-mortgages

A re-mortgage is when you switch your existing mortgage to a new deal — either with your current lender or a new one — without moving house. This can be a "like-for-like" switch onto a better rate, or "further borrowing" to release equity for home improvements, debt consolidation, or other purposes.

Product Transfers

A product transfer means moving onto a new deal with your existing lender, rather than switching lenders entirely. It's usually quicker and involves less paperwork than a re-mortgage, though it can mean fewer options — which is why it's worth checking the whole market first.

Right to Buy

Right to Buy allows eligible council or housing association tenants to buy their home, often at a discount to market value. It differs from a traditional mortgage in that the discount itself can sometimes be used in place of a deposit, and eligibility depends on tenancy length and property type rather than just income and credit history.

Buy to Let

Buy to Let Mortgages

A buy to let mortgage is designed for a property you intend to rent out, not live in yourself. Affordability is assessed differently to a residential mortgage — lenders look mainly at the rent the property can achieve, rather than just your personal income.

Key considerations

How you purchase matters as much as what you purchase. We usually talk through whether to buy in your own name or through a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) company, weighing up tax efficiency against your long-term ambitions — such as growing a wider property portfolio.

Important: it is paramount that you seek independent tax advice before deciding how to structure a buy to let purchase — we're here to arrange the mortgage, not to advise on tax.

Types of Buy to Let
  • 1

    Buy to Let (Residential)

    A standard single let to one household or tenancy.

  • 2

    House of Multiple Occupancy (HMO)

    Let by the room to three or more unrelated tenants sharing facilities.

  • 3

    Holiday Let

    Furnished property let short-term to holidaymakers.

  • 4

    Commercial Let

    Property let to a business tenant rather than a residential occupier.

Commercial Mortgages & Credit

Commercial Mortgages & Credit

Commercial finance suits businesses buying trading premises, investors purchasing commercial property, or developers funding a project. It's best suited to anyone looking to secure a loan against a property that isn't a standard residential home.

Commercial Mortgages

For trading & investment property

A loan secured against a commercial property, whether you're buying premises to trade from or a building to let to a business tenant. Terms are typically assessed on the strength of the business or the rental income.

Bridging Finance

Fast, short-term funding

A short-term loan used to "bridge" a gap — for example, buying a new property before selling an existing one, or moving quickly on an auction purchase.

Development Loans

For building & conversion projects

Finance released in stages against a construction or conversion project, matched to the build timeline from groundworks through to completion.
